## 1. What the law actually creates

**Zakon o elektronskim otpremnicama**, Official Gazette RS 94/2024, adopted 27 November 2024.
**[checked]**

- **eOtpremnica** — a structured document accompanying goods (Art. 2(9)). Article 6 requires
  sender, receiver and transporter identification with tax numbers, waybill number and date,
  planned and actual transport start and end times, loading and unloading addresses, goods
  description and quantity, vehicle identification, and a QR code for verification.
- **ePrijemnica** — a document of **qualitative and quantitative acceptance**, created from the
  received eOtpremnica (Art. 2(10)).
- **Operated by the Centralni informacioni posrednik** inside the Ministry of Finance (Art. 9).
- **Retention:** permanent for the public sector, **ten years** for the private sector (Art. 11).
- **Phases:** 1 January 2026 for the public sector and private traders in excise goods;
  **1 October 2027 for private-sector pairs**, regardless of goods type or VAT status.

### The workflow, which is the part that matters **[my research]**

1. The sender creates and transmits the eOtpremnica **before the goods move**.
2. Once sent, **the document locks** — only attachments and unplanned transshipment stay editable.
3. The recipient confirms physical receipt within three business days.
4. The recipient then has **eight days** to send an ePrijemnica stating **accepted versus received
   quantities, line by line**.
5. Partial acceptance is explicitly supported — the ePrijemnica can carry noted discrepancies or
   defects (Art. 8(3)).
6. The sender then has **30 days to align** with that ePrijemnica. If they do not, the ePrijemnica
   is treated as rejected.
7. **Silence by a private-sector recipient means automatic rejection**, and the February 2026
   rulebook amendment makes the system stamp `automatski odbijeno` after 30 days.

**This is the uncomfortable part.** A locked record, two-sided acceptance, per-line quantity
discrepancies, a structured objection and alignment window, and ten-year retention — free,
mandatory, and carrying legal force no private system can match. That is a large share of what we
were planning to demonstrate.

## 2. The three gaps that survive

### 2.1 There is no correction mechanism after acceptance **[my research — needs a lawyer]**

Article 7(7) allows the sender to cancel an eOtpremnica **only up to confirmation of physical
receipt**. Nothing I found addresses amending a record once it has been accepted.

If that holds, the state system handles the *dispute at the door* and has nothing for what comes
after it: a shortage found during unpacking, a quality problem that appears in three days, a
return, a credit note, a retrospective adjustment. That is precisely our supersede-and-revoke
flow, and it is the strongest remaining argument for the product.

**Caveat I want on the record:** "not addressed in the sources I read" is not the same as "does not
exist". This needs a Serbian lawyer before anyone builds a pitch on it.

### 2.2 The foreign leg is outside the system **[reasoning, and I want this checked]**

**I need to correct something I said earlier: the law does cover cross-border movements.** It
applies to shipments within Serbia or partially crossing Serbian territory (Art. 2(2)), and the
February 2026 rulebook amendment added specific import and export fields. I was wrong to say
otherwise.

The nuance is different, and I think sharper. For **imports**, the amendment instructs the sender
to record the place "where they acquired disposal rights" as the dispatch address and loading
point. That reads as the Serbian record beginning at the moment the Serbian party takes ownership.
A foreign supplier is not an obligated subject under Serbian law and has no account in a Serbian
Ministry of Finance system.

If that is right, then **on an import the record is effectively one-sided** — the Serbian party's
own statement of what arrived, with no counter-signature from whoever dispatched it. The leg where
a discrepancy between what left and what was recorded as arriving would occur is outside the
system entirely.

**That is exactly the shape of the Bosnia story**, and it does not close in October 2027.

### 2.3 The state holds the record, for the state's purpose **[reasoning]**

The Centralni informacioni posrednik sits inside the Ministry of Finance. This is the same pattern
as MADR in Romania: the state occupying the record layer. Two consequences worth separating —
for a Serbian domestic pair, they simply do not need us; but a record built for inspection and tax
control is not automatically a commercial-dispute artifact, and neither counterparty controls
their own copy.

## 3. Does it arrive when the law says?

Stefan's standing question. The evidence so far says **yes, but wobbling** — which is exactly the
pattern that leaves room. **[checked]**

- **December 2025:** new exceptions added — aircraft fuelling at a single airport, goods returned
  on the same vehicle immediately after delivery, clinical trial medicines and devices.
- **December 2025:** the Ministry of Finance announced that **from 1 January to 30 June 2026 it
  will not take errors in submitted eOtpremnice and ePrijemnice into account** — a six-month
  grace period declared before the first phase had even started.
- **February 2026:** the rulebook amended again, adding import and export fields and the automatic
  rejection stamp.
- Paper-format provisions deferred to 1 April 2026.

So the public-sector phase launched with an immediate no-penalty window and has been amended twice
since. That is not collapse, but it is not a system arriving cleanly either. **Vladislav has worked
with the Serbian government and his read on whether the October 2027 private-sector date holds is
worth more than any of this.**

## 4. The pain is real — and there is already an industry in it

I had not checked this category before and I should have. **[my research]**

Retail deduction and dispute management is an established software market. Retailers deduct from
supplier payments for alleged shortages, damages, compliance failures, pricing discrepancies and
freight disputes; suppliers dispute those deductions. Named platforms in 2026 include iNymbus,
HighRadius, STAT Recovery Services, RetailPath, ValenceIntel and Transformance, several of them
now marketing themselves as AI-native.

Two readings, and both matter:

**This validates Vladislav's instinct.** He picked a wedge with a real, expensive, recurring
problem and a software category built around it. That is a better signal than anything in the
evidence-layer research.

**And it means the lane is not empty.** One vendor's published claim is that suppliers relying on
deduction software alone recover **less than half** of what they are owed — so the problem is
imperfectly solved, but it is being worked on by funded companies.

One structural difference worth noticing: that industry is built around **US retailer portals** —
Walmart Retail Link, Target Partners Online, Amazon Vendor Central — and its motion is *claims
recovery after the fact*. Nobody in it appears to be selling *neutral evidence at the moment of
the event*. Whether that distinction is a real opening or just a nicer way of describing the same
job is the thing I cannot settle from a desk. **[reasoning]**

## 5. What this does to the wedge

Stated as "delivery and acceptance evidence in fragmented supply chains", the wedge runs into a
free state system in Serbia from October 2027 and an established software category elsewhere.

Stated more precisely, it survives in three places:

1. **Cross-border legs**, where no single state system binds both parties and the record is
   one-sided.
2. **After acceptance** — corrections, returns, quality problems found later — which the state
   flow appears not to cover.
3. **Where a counterparty is not an obligated subject**, or the movement is exempt.

That is a narrower wedge than Vladislav's document proposes, and I think it is the honest version
of it. It also happens to be exactly where the only real evidence we have — the Bosnia story —
actually sits.

I do not think this kills the direction. I think it names it properly.
